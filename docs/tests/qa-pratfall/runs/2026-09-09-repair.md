## Ordering and deadline repair

The independent replay above could prove that each controlled descendant lock was held when
harness cleanup began, but its recorded release value was sampled only after that cleanup. In 21
controlled repetitions, the parent was pending and the lock was still held before cleanup every
time. The replay therefore established neither a native leak nor correct attribution of lock
release: the harness could turn a still-held lock into `released=true` itself.

The harness now samples lock availability immediately after normal communication completes and
before caller-owned safety cleanup. Cleanup remains in `finally` blocks for successful runs,
assertion failures, communication failures, and deadlines. A regression starts a controlled parent
whose separately sessioned child keeps the lock after the parent exits. It verifies
`released=false`, performs owned-process cleanup, and waits up to the cleanup grace for the child to
exit and the lock to become available. The readiness observation retains its separate two-second
bound, communication and identity probes use `HARNESS_TIMEOUT` (15 seconds), and cleanup uses its
own two-second grace. These are independent bounds rather than one 15-second total wall deadline.
